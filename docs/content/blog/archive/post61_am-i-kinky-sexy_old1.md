## First: What Does "Kinky" Actually Mean?

Before you start asking yourself questions, let's be clear about what we're talking about.

"Kinky" doesn't mean crazy. It doesn't mean broken. It doesn't mean you're secretly a different person than you thought.

Kinky just means: you have sexual or intimate desires that fall outside the baseline of what's considered conventional. That's it. Power dynamics, specific sensations, roleplay, particular scenarios — things that don't feel "normal" but feel *right* to you.

The thing about kinky is: it's not binary. You're not kinky or you're not. Most people exist somewhere on a spectrum. You might be kinky in one specific direction (into bondage, into power dynamics, into a specific scenario) and totally vanilla about everything else. You might be kinky as hell and have never acted on any of it. You might have tried something once and decided it was your jam for life.

The question isn't "Am I kinky enough?" The question is "What do I actually want, and what am I willing to explore?"

## The 8 Questions That Actually Tell You Something

Don't answer these to score points. Answer them honestly. Nobody's keeping track but you.

### Question 1: When you imagine a sexual scenario that really works for you, who is in charge?

This is the one that matters most, because power dynamics are the root of almost everything kinky.

In your ideal moment — not what you think you *should* want, but what actually gets you going — is someone leading? Are *you* leading? Is it equal but someone still makes decisions?

If the answer is "wait, let me think about this for a second" — that's information. Most vanilla people don't need to think about it. They just... happen.

If you notice that someone being in charge is part of what makes it hot for you, you're tracking toward kinky.

### Question 2: In your fantasies, is there usually a story or a structure?

Some people just need two attractive people and chemistry. Other people need a *scenario*. A reason. A setup. A role.

Secretary and boss. Teacher and student. Stranger in a bar. Authority figure. Specific power dynamic.

Vanilla sex can be spontaneous. Kinky sex is often *designed*.

If you can't get aroused unless there's a frame — a reason, a narrative, specific roles — that's a pretty clear signal.

### Question 3: Does the thought of someone taking charge of you feel like relief or threat?

This matters because submission is its own form of power, and not everyone has access to it.

When you imagine someone making decisions *for* you — about what happens, what you do, what you wear, how fast, how slow — does that feel:

- Like relief? ("Finally, someone else decides.")
- Like attraction? ("I want them to want that.")
- Like threat? ("Absolutely not.")
- Like confusion? ("I don't know, maybe?")

Relief and attraction both point toward submission. Threat and confusion suggest it's not your flavor.

### Question 4: Has there ever been a moment — even outside sex — where someone's authority made you feel something?

Think about a moment when someone took charge. A teacher who commanded a room. A partner who made a decision confidently. Someone who knew what they wanted and moved with certainty.

Did you feel drawn to that? Did it create a physical response? Did you notice yourself wanting to follow that person's lead?

That's not about obedience. That's about recognizing something in yourself that responds to authority. That's kinky.

### Question 5: Is there a specific sensation or scenario that you keep coming back to?

Like, you notice it in different contexts. In different partners. In your imagination. A pattern.

Power exchange. Restraint. Impact. Humiliation. Specific words. Specific roles. Psychological intensity. A particular flavor of vulnerability.

If you notice yourself cycling back to the same thing — it's not random — that's a signal. Your sexuality is trying to tell you something about what you need.

### Question 6: Do you find yourself wanting to be *perceived* in a certain way during sex?

Not just wanted, but *seen in a specific frame*.

As someone's possession. As their prize. As their responsibility. As someone to protect. As someone wild that they have to handle. As someone vulnerable who trusts them.

The frame matters. The being *looked at* in that frame matters.

Vanilla sex doesn't usually have this layer. Kinky sex almost always does.

### Question 7: When you think about exploring something new sexually, is your first instinct curiosity or caution?

Curious people tend to be kinky. Not always, but the pattern is real.

If you see something that doesn't appeal to you, your reaction is usually "oh, that's not for me" and you move on. But if something intrigues you, you don't just forget it.

Cautious people need a lot of convincing. Curious people need permission.

Which one is that, for you?

### Question 8: Is there something you've never told a partner because you thought they'd think it was weird?

This is the real one.

Because most of us have something. A fantasy that feels too specific. A desire that feels too much. A scenario that feels weirdly important to us.

If you have that thing? And you've kept it private because you were worried about judgment? You're probably kinky.

## So What Do Your Answers Actually Mean?

If you answered "yes" or "interesting question, I need to think about this" to more than two of these, you're probably kinky in some direction.

If you answered "yes, definitely" to questions 1 or 3, you're almost certainly kinky.

If you have a specific thing you keep coming back to (Question 5), kinky.

Here's what this doesn't mean:

- You have to act on it
- You have to want to act on it tonight
- You have to tell anyone
- You have to change anything about your life

It just means you know something about yourself. And that knowledge is useful.

## Kinky vs. Curious: Is There Actually a Difference?

Yeah, but it's subtle.

**Curious** is: I wonder what that would feel like. Let me try it.

**Kinky** is: I keep imagining this. It matters to me. I want it.

Curious is a question. Kinky is a knowing.

That said — you can be kinky and not explore it. You can be curious and never act on it. You can try something once and realize it wasn't for you. You can discover something new about yourself at 25 or 45 or 65.

The label doesn't matter. The *knowing* matters.

## FAQ

**If I answered yes to some of these questions does that mean I have to do something?**

Nope. You can know something about yourself and choose not to act on it. That's valid. But at least you know.

**What if the answer scares me?**

That's normal. A lot of people are scared by their own desires because they've been told those desires are weird or bad. They're not. They're just information about what you need.

**What if I'm kinky but my partner isn't?**

That's a separate conversation. But knowing what you want is the first step to figuring out if you want it *with them*.

**What if I realized I'm kinky and I've been pretending I'm vanilla my whole relationship?**

Welcome to a very common experience. This is exactly why couples therapy or a coach can help. There's a way to have this conversation that deepens trust instead of breaking it.

**Can you become kinky or stop being kinky?**

You can become *aware* of kinkiness you didn't know you had. You can suppress desires and then rediscover them. But the underlying attraction patterns are pretty stable. If you're kinky, you're kinky. You can choose how to express that, but the desire doesn't just disappear.

**What if none of these questions resonated with me?**

Then you might actually be vanilla, and that's completely fine. Or you might be kinky in a direction these questions didn't touch. Either way, you have information.

**Is there a "right" answer?**

No. This isn't a test. There's no winning. The only right answer is the honest one.
