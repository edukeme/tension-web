## How This Works

This workbook has eight sections. Work through them in order — together, in a single session if you can manage two hours, or across a few evenings if that's more your speed.

What you're NOT supposed to do: fill it in alone and hand it to your partner as a fait accompli. That's not negotiation. That's a presentation. Negotiation means you're both in the room, both uncertain, both figuring it out at the same time.

Keep a pen — two pens — and enough uninterrupted time. No phones. Not in bed right before sleep when one of you is already half-gone. Not right after an intense scene when the emotional weather is too interesting to think clearly.

Sit down like adults who have decided to build something together. Because that's what you're doing.

One more thing before we start: if you don't already have the **Tension BDSM Checklist** (it's free at tensionapp.com/checklist), get it now. You'll need it for Section 2. It's the map. This workbook is the conversation about the map.

## A Last Word from Mr. D

The couples who make it — the ones who tell me their dynamic has been running clean for years, where the surrender is real and the trust goes somewhere deep — they have one thing in common. Not contracts. Not perfect protocols. Not the most extensive checklist.

They have the conversation.

They keep coming back to it. They sit down with honesty instead of assumptions. They say the awkward thing. They update the document when something changes. They treat the review like an appointment that matters — because it does.

What you've built here is a starting point. It will look different in six months. That's not a bug. It means you're both still in it, still paying attention, still choosing each other deliberately.

That's the whole thing, really. The dynamic is just a structure for choosing each other — again, and again, with your eyes open.

Use this well.
