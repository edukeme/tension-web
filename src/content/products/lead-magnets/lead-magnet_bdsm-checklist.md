# How to Use This Checklist

## When to do it

Not in bed. That's the rule.

Do it on a relaxed evening — wine optional, pressure not allowed. The kitchen table works. The couch works. A quiet corner of a restaurant works if you're feeling bold. Anywhere that isn't the bedroom, where everything carries ten times the weight it needs to.

Don't do it right after sex, either. Post-sex brains are either floaty and agreeable (you'll say yes to everything) or wired and self-conscious (you'll shut down). Give yourselves a neutral, low-stakes setting.

Plan for 30–45 minutes the first time through. You might go longer. That's a good sign.

## How to do it

Read each item together — out loud if you're comfortable, silently if you're not. Then each of you marks your response in your column. No peeking until you're both done with a section. Then compare and talk.

The talking is the product. The checklist just gives you something to talk about.

A few ground rules:

- **No judgment, in either direction.** "I can't believe you want that" kills this as fast as "I can't believe you don't want that." Both reactions are banned.
- **Curiosity beats certainty.** You don't have to know how you feel about everything. That's what "Curious" is for.
- **Your answer is about you, not about your partner.** You're not responding to what you think they want. You're saying what's actually true for you.
- **This is a conversation, not a contract.** Nothing on this list is a promise. Checking "Yes" doesn't mean you owe anyone anything. It means you want to have a conversation about it.

## The Five Response Options

You'll use these five responses for every item. Here's what each one means — and a way to say it out loud to your partner.

| Response | What it means | Say it like this |
|----------|---------------|-----------------|
| **Yes** | I want this. I'm in. | *"This is a yes for me. I'd genuinely like to try this."* |
| **Curious** | I don't know yet, but I'm interested in finding out. | *"I've thought about this and I'm curious — I'd want to talk about it more."* |
| **Maybe someday** | Not now, not off the table either. | *"I'm not ready for this right now, but I don't want to close the door."* |
| **Not for us** | No interest, no appeal — and that's completely fine. | *"This just doesn't do anything for me. I'm a pass."* |
| **Hard limit** | No. This is a boundary I need respected, not negotiated. | *"This is a hard no for me. It's not up for discussion, and that's okay."* |

The difference between "Not for us" and "Hard limit" matters. "Not for us" is an aesthetic preference — you're not into it, like not being into cilantro. A hard limit is a real boundary that needs to stay in place. Both are valid. Neither needs an explanation.

## The Golden Rule

**No answer makes you broken. No answer makes you obligated. This is a conversation, not a contract.**

Read that again.

Whatever comes up in this checklist — whatever you check, whatever your partner checks — is just information. It's data about who you are and what you want. There's nothing in here that means something is wrong with you. There's nothing in here that means you're required to do anything you don't want to do.

The goal is understanding each other better. That's it. Everything else — what you actually do with what you learn — is up to you, together, at whatever pace makes sense.

Okay. Let's start.

## Category 7: Aftercare & Coming Down Together

*Here's the part most checklists skip. Whatever happens in a scene — even a light one, even something that's just a bit more intense than usual — your nervous system was activated. Both of you. The dominant and the submissive. Coming down from that activation is as important as the scene itself. Aftercare is how you land.*

*Mr. D's note: "Drop" is real. Sub drop is well-documented — the emotional dip that can happen hours or days after a scene. Dom drop happens too. Neither of you should be alone with it. # What Do Your Answers Tell You?

So you've gone through it. You've compared. Now what?

Here's how to read what you found — not as a scoring system, but as a map.

---

## If you have a lot of matches

You're already more aligned than most couples admit to being. The work now isn't convincing each other — it's deciding where to start. Pick one or two things in the "Yes / Yes" column that you haven't tried yet. Start there. Small, deliberate, with a plan.

The mistake at this stage is doing too much too fast because you're excited. You found the map. Now travel it carefully.

---

## If you have a lot of "Curious / Curious" overlap

This is actually the best starting position. Neither of you is sure, both of you are interested. That's called exploration, and it's where the most interesting things happen.

Pick the item you're both most curious about. Research it together. Watch something, read something, talk about what specifically is interesting about it. Then decide if you want to try it. The conversation is the first step, not the last one.

---

## If you have some significant mismatches

One of you marked Yes. The other marked Hard Limit. Or you both marked something opposite.

First: this is normal. Perfectly compatible couples have mismatches on this checklist. A mismatch is not incompatibility — it's information.

The rule is simple: a Hard Limit is a full stop. It doesn't get negotiated, reasoned around, or pressured. It gets respected. Full stop.

For softer mismatches — one person enthusiastic, the other "maybe someday" — the conversation is: *what would need to be true for this to move from maybe to yes?* Or: *can I be happy knowing this is something we're not going to do?* Both questions are worth asking. Neither has a wrong answer.

---

## If you're feeling overwhelmed

You found a lot of things you want, or a lot of things you didn't know about yourself, or a gap you didn't expect. That's a lot to hold.

Don't try to process it all in one sitting. Close the checklist. Be with each other. Come back to it tomorrow.

The checklist isn't going anywhere. Neither are you.
