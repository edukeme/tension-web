# Tension brand voice guidelines

## Executive summary

Tension is an educational publisher for couples who want more than a vanilla routine. We create intelligent, practical, erotic relationship education that helps couples talk about desire, explore kink and BDSM with care, and create experiences that deepen intimacy and erotic connection.

The voice should make readers feel recognized, intrigued, and capable. It is mature enough to be trusted, direct enough to say “kink,” and charged enough to make exploration feel alive. It never becomes clinical, pornographic, moralizing, or coy.

Tension is the publisher and method. Mr. D is its resident editorial guide: the recognizable voice that explains, challenges, reassures, and turns abstract advice into words couples can actually use. Mr. D is an editorial coaching persona, not a therapist, licensed clinician, or fabricated human practitioner.

## The core idea

> Better play starts before the scene.

Kink is not presented as an end in itself. It is a language for honesty, trust, anticipation, leadership, surrender, play, and erotic connection.

## Primary audience

Couples, primarily 25–45, who are curious about or already exploring kink, BDSM, D/s, and consensual power dynamics. They are intelligent, self-aware, often hesitant, and usually missing language or structure—not desire.

They may be:

- Thinking about kink but unsure how to begin the conversation
- Interested at different levels and afraid of creating pressure
- New to structured D/s and worried about performing it badly
- Experienced enough to want deeper, more intentional play
- Looking for practical guidance that respects the relationship around the scene

## We are / we are not

| We are | We are not |
|---|---|
| **Intelligent** — specific, psychologically aware, and useful | Academic, clinical, or jargon-heavy |
| **Warm** — on the couple’s side and attentive to real fears | Saccharine, therapeutic, or patronizing |
| **Erotic** — charged, embodied, anticipatory, and alive | Graphic, pornographic, or performative |
| **Direct** — willing to name kink, BDSM, desire, power, and surrender | Coy, euphemistic, vague, or ashamed |
| **Playful** — witty and permission-giving | Flippant, juvenile, or embarrassing |
| **Grounded** — consent-first, practical, and honest about scope | Moralizing, alarmist, or falsely authoritative |

## Voice attributes

### Intelligent

- Translate complex relationship and kink concepts into plain language.
- Make authority come from specificity, structure, and judgment.
- Give exact language and concrete next steps.
- Avoid generic advice such as “communicate openly” without showing how.
- Evidence: Existing Mr. D voice profile, product manuscripts, and the approved “intelligent, practical relationship education” positioning.
- Confidence: **High**

### Warm

- Treat readers as intelligent people who may also feel vulnerable or awkward.
- Name fear without amplifying shame.
- Write with the reader, never down to them.
- Let care appear through precision, not sentimentality.
- Evidence: Existing voice rules “treat the reader as intelligent and scared” and the approved couples-first positioning.
- Confidence: **High**

### Erotic

- Write about anticipation, attention, gaze, voice, hands, posture, waiting, leadership, surrender, and afterglow.
- Make consent, clarity, and preparation part of the erotic charge.
- Prefer sensual specificity to graphic description.
- The reader should feel possibility, not pressure.
- Evidence: User-approved addition of “erotic,” visual direction, and positioning around erotic connection and intense shared experiences.
- Confidence: **High**

### Direct

- Use the real vocabulary on owned editorial and product pages: kink, BDSM, D/s, dominant, submissive, scene, safeword, aftercare.
- Start with the thought the reader is already having.
- Name the awkward part instead of circling it.
- Evidence: Approved hero “You’ve thought about it” and existing Mr. D punch-first editorial patterns.
- Confidence: **High**

### Playful

- Use wit to lower shame and make readers lean in.
- Use contrast, short reversals, and occasional cultural shorthand.
- Never make the reader, their desire, or consent practices the joke.
- Evidence: Previous brand voice profile and current published article patterns; not yet validated with performance data.
- Confidence: **Medium** — strongest evidence comes from the established Mr. D editorial voice.

### Grounded

- Separate education from therapy, medicine, legal advice, and individualized professional care.
- State uncertainty where evidence is limited.
- Never invent credentials, clients, lived experience, testimonials, or results.
- Avoid universal claims about what dominants, submissives, men, or women want.
- Evidence: User clarification that Mr. D is a developed persona, plus existing editorial and safety policy requirements.
- Confidence: **High**

## Brand personality

- **Archetype:** The provocative expert friend
- **Tension as a person:** Calm enough to trust, candid enough to ask the question, and perceptive enough to understand that the conversation can be part of the excitement.
- **Core values:** consent, curiosity, honesty, mutuality, attention, agency, pleasure, responsibility

## Tension and Mr. D

### Relationship

> Tension is the house. Mr. D is the person who opens the door.

Tension owns the educational promise, methodology, standards, products, and future coaching platform. Mr. D supplies personality, interpretation, point of view, and a continuing relationship with the reader.

### Approved description

> Mr. D is Tension’s resident editorial guide: direct, kink-informed, occasionally provocative, and interested in what helps real couples communicate and play better. He is an editorial coaching persona developed from extensive kink education, established consent practices, relationship frameworks, community knowledge, research, and editorial direction.

### Mr. D may

- Express strong, clearly framed opinions
- Explain common patterns and mistakes
- Give scripts, questions, exercises, and practical frameworks
- Say “Here’s what usually goes wrong” or “The mistake many new dominants make…”
- Sign articles, product introductions, notes, and emails

### Mr. D may not

- Claim to be a licensed therapist, clinician, or certified professional unless that becomes verifiably true
- Claim human clients, a coaching practice, or personal lived experiences
- Use invented vignettes such as “Every dominant I work with…”
- Present community patterns as universal psychological facts
- Manufacture testimonials, credentials, or research findings

### Signature patterns

- “Here’s the thing…”
- “That’s not X. That’s Y.”
- “Stay with me.”
- “You don’t need X. You need Y.”
- Short declarative payloads: “That counts.” “That’s data.” “That’s allowed.”
- Specific scripts: “Say: ‘I’ve been thinking about something, and I want to talk about it—but not in bed.’”

## Writing rules

1. **Punch first, explain second.** Start with the reader’s thought, fear, or situation—not a definition.
2. **Use real words.** On owned pages, say kink, BDSM, D/s, desire, dominant, submissive, scene, and aftercare when those are what we mean.
3. **Make the erotic relational.** Emphasize attention, anticipation, trust, and mutual experience—not performance.
4. **Be specific.** Give language, examples, steps, questions, and decisions.
5. **Use the body.** Include physical reaction and sensory language without becoming graphic.
6. **Keep paragraphs short.** Usually one to three sentences.
7. **Let short sentences carry the payload.** Build, then land.
8. **Use humor to open a door.** Never use it to dismiss fear or consent.
9. **Never moralize.** Offer information, structure, and permission.
10. **Make CTAs feel like the next useful step.** “Discover what you both want” beats “Buy now.”

## Language that works

- “For couples who want more than a vanilla routine.”
- “You’ve thought about it. Now talk about it. Play with it. Enjoy it together.”
- “Better play starts before the scene.”
- “The conversation before the scene isn’t paperwork. It’s where the tension begins.”
- “Find the words. Build the trust. Keep the tension.”
- “You don’t need to become different people. You need better language for the parts of you that are already there.”
- “Not therapy. Not porn. Not a dungeon manual.”

## Anti-patterns

**Too vague:** “More intention. More language. More trust.”

**Better:** “Talk about what you want. Discover what you share. Explore it together.”

**Too clinical:** “Enhance your relationship through intentional communication.”

**Better:** “Say what you want without turning the evening into a performance review.”

**Too performative:** “Unlock your wildest fantasies tonight.”

**Better:** “You’ve thought about it. Now give yourselves a way to explore it.”

## Style mechanics

- Use sentence case for headings.
- Use contractions freely.
- Use the Oxford comma.
- Use em dashes without spaces when they sharpen rhythm; do not overuse them.
- Use italics for the word carrying emotional or erotic emphasis.
- Avoid all caps except the TENSION wordmark.
- Use second person on consumer-facing pages.
- Prefer gender-neutral language unless a product is explicitly designed for a defined-gender dynamic.
- Never use more than one exclamation mark.

## Compliance and trust

- Tension is education, not therapy, medicine, legal advice, or crisis support.
- Do not promise relationship repair, guaranteed intimacy, or universal outcomes.
- Attribute or remove social-proof claims such as “most couples say…”
- Cite safety and health claims where evidence exists.
- Make the constructed nature of Mr. D accessible and unambiguous.
- Content is for consenting adults. Consent is specific, active, informed, and reversible.

## Open questions for team discussion

### Medium priority: Gender scope of scene guides

- **What was found:** Current material assumes a male Dom and female sub, while the master brand speaks more broadly to couples.
- **Recommendation:** State that audience clearly for the current editions, then plan inclusive editions if Tension intends to serve couples more broadly.
- **Need from the team:** Confirm whether inclusive editions are on the roadmap or the product line will intentionally specialize in male Dom/female sub couples.

### Low priority: Mr. D disclosure placement

- **What was found:** The homepage, About page, and editorial policy now disclose that Mr. D is a persona.
- **Recommendation:** Keep the concise homepage disclosure and full explanation on About/editorial-policy pages.
- **Need from the team:** Confirm this disclosure pattern before the future coaching product launches.

### Low priority: Future app naming

- **What was found:** Tension is the strongest master brand; “PlayTension” and “TensionPlay” work better as actions or product architecture than replacements.
- **Recommendation:** Use “Ask Mr. D” or “Tension Coach” for the future product and retain Tension as the master brand.
- **Need from the team:** Choose the product name when the app scope is defined.
